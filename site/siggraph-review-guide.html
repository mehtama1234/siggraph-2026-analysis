Review Rule

Each article should explain one hidden quantity in ordinary terms before naming techniques. For graphics, the hidden quantity is usually connection, conservation, light transport, material response, time continuity, perception, manufacturability, control, or failure boundary.

Primary Claims To Inspect

Shape

Surface truth should teach why a visible surface needs preserved connections, inside-out tests, distances, and correspondence.

Physics

Simulation balance should teach why time stepping is bookkeeping for mass, contact, momentum, and constraints.

Motion

Motion state should teach why animation is state carried through time, not independent poses.

Materials

Material response should connect appearance, force, fibers, scattering, and deformation.

Light

Light accounting should make rendering feel like adding possible paths, not drawing pixels.

Generation

Generative constraints should explain why invention must preserve shape, view, identity, and editability.

Display

Display and eye should distinguish hardware limits from perceptual tolerance.

Trust

Failure boundary should say what the result does not prove.

Example Strength Audit

Display

The display examples should prove a limit of hardware, eye response, latency, color, focus, brightness, or holographic reconstruction. Avoid examples that are only about identity or relighting.

Fabrication

The fabrication examples should prove that geometry must pass through material, toolpath, deployability, stress, thickness, or manufacturing limits. Avoid examples that only describe visual appearance.

Sound And Touch

The sound-contact examples should keep the event cause visible: contact, deformation, vibration, haptic proxy, cloth motion, or mechanical response.

Learning

The learning examples should show a learned shortcut preserving the rule it replaces: a solver, field, controller, material model, or physical force.
