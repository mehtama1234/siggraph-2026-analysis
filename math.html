A small toolkit, used everywhere.

Computer graphics looks like many different crafts — cloth, light, faces, fluids, shapes. Underneath, nearly all of it runs on the same short list of mathematical ideas. This is that list, each idea explained in plain words, with every 2026 paper placed under the one it leans on and a one-line note on the math it actually uses.

How do you turn the messy, continuous, physical world into numbers a computer can balance, minimise, add up, and predict?

That is what all of this math is for. A shape, a splash of water, a lit scene — each has to become a problem of a familiar mathematical shape before a computer can touch it. Read the ideas below in order and you'll see the same handful of moves recur across wildly different papers: balance everything at once, find the least-bad arrangement, step forward in time, add up an infinity of small things, sample cleverly, measure how things curve, find their natural patterns, and — increasingly — learn the rule from examples. A whole field, on one small toolkit.

Finding a thing's natural patterns

Every shape and every image is secretly built from a set of natural patterns — the basic ways a bell likes to ring, the coarse-to-fine layers of detail in a picture. Uncover those few underlying patterns and you can smooth, shrink, or animate a thing by working with its handful of important modes instead of all its raw data. The big-picture problem: raw data is bulky and noisy; its natural patterns are few and clean.

Measuring the shape of a shape

Before you can smooth, bend, or decorate a surface, you have to measure its local geometry — how sharply it curves here, how it stretches there, which way it faces. This is the mathematics of curvature: treating a surface not as a bag of points but as something with real, measurable geometric properties at every spot. Almost everything you'd do to a shape begins by measuring how it curves.

Better Bending: Analysis, Construction and Verification of Discrete Bending Models for Kirchhoff-Love Shells
Thin shells bend and stretch; the method measures bending by comparing how surfaces curve at neighboring points. Finding a realistic shape means minimizing the strain energy where bending is captured as discrete angle changes across the 3D model.
Persistence-guided Prescribed Topological Simplification
Topological features like holes persist across different scales; the method removes only weak features by cutting or filling while preserving those marked as important, using persistence to rank which features matter most.
The PhaseTree: Multiphase Signed Distance Fields
Multi-material objects are represented as signed distance fields—numbers encoding how far any point is from the nearest surface—combined through geometric transformations; the system extends ray tracing to handle these layered distance measurements.
Surface Power Diagrams for Knit Singularity Placement
Knit structures need singularities positioned to avoid helical twists. Use power diagrams (regions of equal generalized distance) on 3D model surfaces to optimally place singularities by iteratively moving each toward the curl center of its region.
Robust Computation of Boundary Path Integrals Using Kernel-Density Estimation
When light interacts with object boundaries during rendering, you must add up infinitely many light paths bouncing at the edge—a mathematical integral. By smearing the razor-thin boundary slightly with a smooth kernel (like photon mapping does), this integral becomes easier to estimate accurately without sampling exactly on the edge.
A Unified Homogenization Framework for Straight- and Curved-Crease Origami Materials
Origami materials (folded sheets) have unusual stiffness and bending because creases redirect strain. Classical Laminate Theory calculates effective stiffness from fold angles and surface curvature; simulation folds the crease pattern, then homogenization averages the behavior to predict macroscopic properties from microscopic fold geometry.
Guidestar-Free Adaptive Optics with Asymmetric Apertures
Light aberrations bend unpredictably through an asymmetric aperture, and the resulting patterns encode the distortions; machine learning decodes these patterns to infer what the aberrations are, then optics corrects them.
A Two-Millisecond Passthrough Headset for Perceptual Studies
End-to-end latency measures the delay between real head motion and display update; perceptual studies measure how human perception changes as this delay shrinks, revealing threshold sensitivities in real tasks.
Woodstock: Interactive Modeling of Fungal Wood Decay
Wood decay spreads via fungal colonization and moisture image-generating; simulate how fungi consume material (changing local stiffness), how water flows anisotropically through grain, and how cracks grow when stress exceeds weakened tissue.
Uncertainty-aware geometry processing on Gaussian Process Implicit Surfaces
A probabilistic surface model (Gaussian Process) represents uncertain geometry as a cloud of plausible shapes; compute surface operators (slope, curl, curvature) by averaging over that cloud, weighting by how likely each shape is.
Spatially Accelerated Winding Numbers for Curved Geometry
Winding numbers tell if a point is inside curved geometry by measuring rotation around it; precompute moment summaries in a spatial tree, then use Taylor approximations for distant curves and exact integration for nearby ones.
Fast and Exact Winding Numbers for Triangle Meshes
Winding numbers measure net rotation around a point to test if it's inside; reduce the surface integral analytically to a single ray-3D model test plus a sum over boundary edges, orders of magnitude faster than prior methods.
MorphSkein: A Shape-Changing Afterimage Display Preserving Pixel Density During Surface-Area Changes Across Troposkein-Based Shapes
Spinning cables form natural axisymmetric shapes governed by physics. The display maps pixels to those mathematical shapes, preserving density by adjusting how many pixels render per revolution as the shape expands.
Mechanical Cloaking of Halftoned Imagery
A porous material's internal structure is designed so its effective mechanical properties (how it bends and stretches as a whole) are uniform in all directions, while its visual appearance halftones a target image. Balancing visual appearance against uniform stiffness is the core constraint.
A Precision Controlled Surface-Surface Intersection Algorithm for NURBS
Finding where two complex surfaces intersect and controlling precision means bounding how far the true curve strays from our approximation using curvature measures and geometric hulls that must all satisfy distance limits at once.
Points as Tori: Fast Pointwise Signed Distance for Point Clouds
Signed distance to point clouds is computed by fitting each point with a torus (which has a closed-form distance formula), using a a system that learns from examples to predict local curvature, enabling fast queries without global optimization.
Constant Mean Curvature Surfaces from Discrete Harmonic Maps
Constant mean curvature surfaces (elegant shapes with balanced bending) are built by discretizing the classical fact that their normal maps are harmonic—i.e., as smooth as possible—solved via energy minimization and Poisson equations.

Adding up an infinity of tiny contributions

The color of a single pixel is really the total of all the light arriving there from every direction — a continuous infinity of contributions. The mass of a blob, the total force on a surface: the same shape of problem, a sum over something continuous. You can never add up infinitely many things exactly, so the whole game is approximating these totals accurately and cheaply — the quiet math behind turning light into a picture.

GenPIE: A Time-Resolved Plenoptic Imager
Light bounces off surfaces and through the scene in complex paths; the method uses a generative model to predict missing light paths when sensors cannot directly observe them, then refines these guesses by tracking how light actually flows.
Gradient Domain Reconstruction for Monte Carlo PDE Solvers
Solving Poisson equations with random rays is slow because the answer wiggles wildly. Instead of estimating the solution itself, estimate how it changes locally (its slope), then reconstruct the answer more stably from those differences.
Schrödinger Bridges on Discretized Geometric Domains
Interpolate between two probability distributions by finding the smoothest path between them via entropy-regularized transport. The heat equation reveals this path; apply Sinkhorn iterations (alternating local averaging and scaling) to enforce boundary constraints.
Sample Matching for Joint Extinction Gradient Estimation in Differentiable Volume Rendering
Rendering smoke requires summing light arriving along rays, but computing how that sum changes with smoke density is noisy. Evaluate both scattering and transmission effects at matching sample points along the same ray segment to cancel out their wild swings.
ToF ReSTIR: Time-of-Flight Rendering with Spatio-temporal Reservoir Resampling
Time-gated imaging traces light bounces that arrive within a narrow time window; simulating all bounces is expensive. Reuse previously computed bounces by warping them (via Newton's method) to satisfy the time constraints, then resample intelligently.
HoloPathTracer: Fast and Accurate Wave Path Tracing for Holography
Holography encodes wavefronts (which waves go where) into a phase pattern—this is an integral over all light paths, analogous to rendering. Monte Carlo path tracing randomly samples paths and weights them to estimate this integral; path reuse (recycling samples) dramatically cuts the number of simulations needed.
Thermal Non-Line-of-Sight Imaging through Rough Surfaces
Hidden scenes emit thermal radiation that bounces through rough walls to a camera—reconstructing what's hidden requires reversing this complex light transport. The a system that learns from examples learns the convolution kernel that describes how light scatters, then inverts it to recover the hidden scene from measurements.
Tensor Decomposition-Based Four-dimensional Background-Oriented Schlieren Tomography for High-Speed, High-Fidelity Flow Field Reconstruction
A 4D tensor (space and time) is decomposed into simpler components, then a system that learns from examples refine the solution by tracing how light rays bend through the flow field. Back-propagation updates both the tensor factors and network weights iteratively.
The Antipodal Method: Fast, Accurate, and Robust 3D Generalized Winding Numbers
Winding numbers (counting ray-surface intersections) are expressed as a boundary integral over the surface projected onto a sphere. This geometric insight avoids expensive surface integrals and enables fast, precise queries on both 3D models and parametric surfaces.
Hi-SPAD: Video-Rate Hyperspectral Imaging and Inference with Single-Photon Cameras
Single-photon detectors count random arrival times; the method reconstructs full spectra and materials by treating each detection event as a probabilistic sample. Error bounds decrease exponentially as more photon counts accumulate.
Lifting Lines and Tone: Image-Space Stylization in Path-Space
Light bounces and bends as it travels through a scene; the method adds up all those paths and their effects to decide where to draw stylized lines and shading that look hand-drawn yet follow real physics.
Gaussian Point Splatting
Many tiny opaque points sample from each large Gaussian blur, splatting to pixels in parallel. The method solves how many points to use so their random hits blend to match the original sharp rendering.
Closed-Form Convolution for physically-accurate defocus in Gaussian Splatting
Lens blur spreads light according to aperture shape; the method convolves each 3D Gaussian with a blur kernel using a closed mathematical formula, enabling sharp reconstruction from blurry photos.
Monte Carlo Rendering of Biharmonic Diffusion Curves
Rendering smooth vector graphics with a fourth-order equation (biharmonic) by treating it as a randomized inverse problem: formulate unknown boundary conditions as an energy to minimize, then estimate slope stochastically by sampling random walks on two coupled second-order equations.
Efficient Fur and Hair Multiple Scattering Using Volumetric Approximation
Light bouncing many times through dense hair is expensive to track; instead, treat the hair as a participating medium that scatters light, and stochastically sample one scattering event to approximate higher-order bounces while using explicit fiber geometry for direct lighting detail.
Differentiable Neutron Transport
Neutron transport is computed by summing light arriving from all possible paths through matter; the paper uses random sampling to estimate how each path changes with respect to design choices, then follows the slope downhill to optimize shielding and material layouts.

Smart randomness

When there are far too many possibilities to check them all — every path a ray of light might take, every plausible arrangement — you sample a well-chosen few at random and average the results, trusting the average to land near the true answer. The art is aiming your limited samples where they matter most, so the estimate sharpens quickly instead of staying speckled with noise. It's how graphics tames problems that are, strictly, infinite.

A Few-Step Generative Model on Cumulative Flow Maps
Instead of many refinement steps, this method transports probability distributions in a few giant jumps. It learns cumulative paths that carry a starting distribution toward final images in one or two leaps, reducing computation while keeping quality.
Bounding Stratified Bernoulli Impulses for Ray Marching Gaussian Process Implicit Surfaces
Rendering probabilistic implicit surfaces requires many noisy evaluations; the method computes upper and lower bounds to skip evaluations in empty regions and skip noisy samples when bounds prove no intersection, accelerating rendering.
Stochastic geomorphological transport for terrain erosion simulation
Terrain erosion is simulated by tracking particles (water, sediment) flowing downhill over time; randomness in particle motion captures the chaotic transport that larger deterministic forces miss, producing natural river and delta patterns.

NILE: Nested Interleaving of Low-Dimensional Elements
Ray tracing needs sample points scattered strategically across pixel coordinates so light integrates accurately without aliasing. NILE interleaves local low-dimensional samplers (clustered within pixels) with global high-dimensional sequences (uniform across many pixels), letting you control both.

A physics-simulated character learns to copy human motion (imitation) while also learning to adapt to new environments via trial-and-error (reinforcement learning). The hybrid approach balances likelihood that the motion matches reference data against likelihood of success in novel settings, sharing learned patterns across both objectives.
DMP: Directable Motion Retargeting through Motion Paraphrasing
A image-generating model learns the space of human motions by slowly adding and removing noise from motion examples, learning how to generate new realistic motions. During inference, masked positions guide the generation to satisfy user-specified constraints like joint positions.

Stepping the world forward in time

To animate matter you rarely have a formula for where everything ends up. What you have is the rule for how things change from one instant to the next — this push, this flow, this collision. So you march forward in tiny time-steps, applying the rule again and again, and watch the motion emerge. The whole craft of simulation is taking the largest steps you can without the march spiralling into nonsense.

Volume-Preserving LBM-MPM Coupling for Air-Water-Sand Mixtures
Sand, water, and air interact as they flow and settle; the method splits this into two coupled methods—one that updates fluid motion at each moment, one that tracks how sand grains deform and move. Both proceed in lockstep, exchanging forces at each time step.
Kinetic Predicted-Moment Flux Reconstruction for High-Order High-Performance Fluid Simulation
Fluids evolve as billions of jostling gas molecules; simulating each is impossible. Track instead the aggregate statistics (momentum, energy) at each point via kinetic theory, advancing those aggregates forward in time via the Boltzmann equation.
An Extended Full GKS Formulation for High-Efficiency and Low-Memory Two-Phase Flow Simulation
Two-phase flows (oil-water) require tracking both fluid momentum and phase boundary motion simultaneously. Couple gas-kinetic moment evolution with a phase-field equation that diffuses the interface, advancing both forward stably over time.
Autoregressive Diffusion with Hybrid Representation for Interactive Human Motion Generation
Generate motion one frame at a time, conditioning on text prompts and future poses; a hybrid representation splits the problem (root trajectory separately from body pose) so text and keyframes guide generation together.
Mixed Material Point Methods for Stiff Elastoplasticity
Stiff elastic materials resist deformation; the methods must advance time accurately without requiring impossibly small steps. It reformulates the problem as a saddle-point equation where forces and deformations are solved together implicitly.
Nexus: Native Mesh Generation with Diffusion
A 3D model starts noisy and gradually becomes clearer, step by step, like a photograph developing. At each step, the model predicts which shapes fit the data better, then refines the guess.
Semantic-Structural Alignment for Generative Pictorial Charts
The system generates a chart by repeatedly refining noisy colors, nudged by two signals: what words say it should show, and the structure it must follow. Each refinement step brings it closer to both goals at once.
Progressing Level-of-Detail Animation for Volumetric Elastodynamics
Simulating jiggly volumetric materials at multiple detail levels simultaneously: coarse motion captures bulk deformation, while high-frequency detail is split and recombined with finer resolutions to add bouncy wrinkles without multiplying the number of time-steps needed.
MOCHI: Motion Enhancement of Collaborative Human-object Interactions
Noisy multi-human hand-object interaction captures are refined in two stages: first solve an optimization to find hand grasps that are both physically valid and match the body pose, then evolve the full-body motion forward in time using a image-generating model guided by single-person motion priors.
DiffSurFlow: Efficient and Robust Differentiable Fluid Optimization via Surrogate Strategy on Flow Map
Fluid motion is optimized by following the slope that lowers error, but the paper shortcuts the backward pass by noticing that the flow map's cross-step connections carry slope information far more efficiently than computing every intermediate step's contribution.
DealMaTe: Multi-Dimensional Material Transfer via Diffusion Transformer
The method evolves an image step-by-step from noise toward a realistic material by learning patterns from training examples. It uses depth and lighting information as constraints that guide the transformation, ensuring the output matches the target material's appearance under different conditions.

Finding the least-bad arrangement

A remarkable share of graphics reduces to one move: invent a single number that measures how wrong a configuration is — how stretched the cloth, how far a shape sits from its target, how ugly a layout — then search for the arrangement that makes that number as small as possible, often while certain conditions must stay exactly satisfied. The usual way to search is to feel which small change lowers the number and keep stepping downhill until you can't do better. Score it, then minimise: this runs quietly under fitting, shaping, and much of learning.

The algorithm solves one giant set of coupled equations (one per grid point) to find that shape, using clever resampling so the methods doesn't bog down when there are many particles.

Connecting the dots

Different worlds, the same few moves

Line the ideas up and the surprise is how few there are. A cloth simulation and a shape-fitting method look nothing alike, yet both come down to finding the least-bad arrangement. A water splash and a stress test both march forward in time. A rendered image and a total force both add up an infinity of tiny contributions. The worlds differ; the mathematical moves repeat.

And one move is spreading across all the others. Learning a rule from examples — the newest tool — is now the most common of all, but look closely and it rarely replaces the older math; it sits on top of it. A learned method still fills in between points, still measures how a surface curves, still minimises an error. The 2026 story isn't that graphics stopped being mathematical — it's that a learned layer now rides on the same small toolkit the field has always used.

Every believable pixel and every convincing motion is one of a few mathematical questions in disguise: what balances, what is least, what comes next, what adds up — and, now, what can be learned. Master those few and the whole field opens up.
